# Ground Crew Study Guide

## I. Airport Setup

**Origin Airports:** Firstly, use the time provided in the briefing to track the airport we are departing from and check the airlines and aircraft operating there. Then, start setting up the airport with the data you received. Set up one zone for the home airline (based on the airport's country). You can gather airport setup information on FlightRadar 24.

**Destination Airports:** Same as setting up for the origin airport, you should repeat the same with the destination airport too. After that, leave a vacant stand/gate and bring up some ground crew vehicles. It is not required to use all ground crew vehicles — only a stair truck, fuel truck, and baggage truck are okay. Note: only start this when the plane has taken off.

## II. Aircraft Setup

**Information:** This is the most important part of the flight as Ground Crew. The cones and ground crew vehicles should be placed in their correct positions, and all operations must be carried out correctly. The next slides will show you how.

### Baggage Truck

**What is it:** A vehicle used for transporting luggage to the plane

**When to use:** Check-in is completed, boarding has started, and it is removed when boarding is completed

**Where to place:** At the right back part of the plane for all planes, and an additional one at the front right part of the plane if using widebody planes

**How often:** Every flight must have it

### Catering Truck

**What is it:** A vehicle used for providing services like food and drinks

**When to use:** During check-in, and removed when boarding is completed

**Where to place:** Front right door, and 1 additional at the right back door if using widebodies

**How often:** Flights that are over 1 hour long

### Stair Truck

**What is it:** A vehicle used to help passengers board the plane if not using the gate, or for staff to get on the plane

**When to use:** When boarding starts (on the bus) and only for flights that do not use a jetway, or check-in, and removed when boarding is completed.

**Where to place:** Front left door, and 1 additional at the left back door if using widebodies (if not using jetway), or one at the left back door if using jetway.

**How often:** Every time for flights that do not use a jetway, or are in a stand

### Fuel Truck

**What is it:** A vehicle used to supply fuel to the plane

**When to use:** Check-in starts, and removed when boarding is completed

**Where to place:** Next to the right wing, not under

**How often:** Every flight, and use a large fuel truck for widebodies and a small one for narrow-body planes

### Terminal Bus

**What is it:** A vehicle used for moving passengers to board the plane if not using the gate

**When to use:** Drive to bus stand when check-in starts, and drive away when everyone has left the plane

**Where to place:** Bus stand

**How often:** Every time for flights that do not use a jetway, or are in a stand

### Pushback Tug

**What is it:** A vehicle used for pushing back the plane — use the long pushback tow or pushback tug, and avoid using the small one except if it is a small plan.e

**When to use:** All cones and ground crew vehicles are removed

**Where to place:** Front of the plane, heading the same direction as the plane, for easy connection

**How often:** Every time for flights — ask the pilot for tail heading directions. Do not turn too soon, as it will make it unrealistic, and avoid colliding with signage. Align the front gear with the taxi line and do not turn early. Disconnect the aircraft once you finish the pushback and drive it away, rather than despawning it midway

## III. Cone Placement

Cone placement is necessary because it will mark the zones that we should not go to. We will place cones like this because it applies to all planes. Place the cone for departure in the prep time and remove it before departure. Only place the cones when the engine has stopped operating.
