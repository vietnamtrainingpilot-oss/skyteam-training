# Flight Deck Study Guide

## I. Flight Plan

**Information:** After the briefing, file a flight plan with the flight operator. You should read and understand the flight plan.

### Example Format

- **Callsign:** LX001 - flight callsign
- **Aircraft:** A320 - type of aircraft
- **Departure:** HAN - where we take off
- **Arrival:** SGN - where we land
- **Route:** GPS Direct - what type we use during flight
- **Flight Level:** 030 - flight altitude
- **Runway:** IRFD 25L ITKO 31 - departure and landing runway

## II. Plane-Checking

**Information:** This part has no purpose, but it makes the flight operation more realistic. One of the 2 crew decks should go down and walk around the aircraft. The main things to look at are the gears, flaps, tail, and other important parts of the plane.

## III. Taxi

**Pushback:** Before pushing back, you must provide the ground crew with the direction of pushing back. Do not turn on the engine during pushback when it is connected, because of a bug in PTFS. Wait for the ground crew to finish the pushback.

**Taxi:** After pushback, you may start up the engines. I recommend looking at the chart since it would help you taxi much better with planned routes. The taxi speed should remain between 10 -20 knots because the safety demonstration video is long. Try to stay on the centerline of the taxiway.

## IV. Takeoff

When you are taking off, make sure to set the throttle to 80% and try to take off as smoothly as possible. Do not pitch too much and remain in gear until you get to 200 ft so you can deploy the gear. When the plane reaches 200 kts, retract the flaps. Also, the speed should remain under 250 kts until you reach the cruising altitude. From that point, you may set the speed as high as you can, but realistically, set it around 85-95 percent.

## V. Cruising

During the climb, do not make sharp turns. Before leveling off, ensure the aircraft is pointed toward your destination. As you reach the cruising altitude based on your flight plan, begin a smooth transition to level flight. Don't be too picky in correcting, as being within ± 50 feet of your assigned altitude is acceptable. Once you've reached the cruising altitude, press `R` to keep the altitude. The aircraft will continue flying on the heading you've selected. When you're halfway through the route, press `F` to enable cruise mode for in-flight service. Be careful not to disable cruise mode accidentally, as doing so could cause passengers to fall out. Notify the cabin crew that they may begin meal service. Only turn off cruise mode after the cabin crew has confirmed they are seated and the flight service has been completed.

## VI. Descent

When descending, keep your descent rate realistic. The aircraft should not look like it is diving. **Do NOT** pitch down to reach the runway or capture the glideslope. If you are too high, fly a circular descent or perform a go-around instead. Keep your turns smooth and avoid steep bank angles. Make small adjustments to stay lined up with the runway.

## VII. Landing

When landing, use the PAPI lights to indicate how high you currently are, as this will determine your touchdown. That being said, try to touch down within the touchdown zone to avoid floating in the air and causing an overrun. Check if you are currently at the correct landing speed and flaps configuration.

After touchdown, lower the nose wheel normally and do not keep it raised for too long. Once all wheels are on the ground, apply reverse thrust at about 40% throttle. When your speed reaches 60 knots, turn off reverse thrust and use taxi thrust instead. Find the nearest exit. Exit the runway at a maximum of 30 kts, and retract the flaps once you have reached the taxiway.

## VIII. Taxi to Gate

It is just the same as normal taxi. When you are ready to get to the gate, follow the marshaller's instructions. Once you have finished, shut down your engines and wait for all passengers to deboard the plane.
